About

Four years of coursework, eight months of real data.

Most reporting problems are not a shortage of data. There is too much of it, in too many places. Sales in one sheet, ads in another, a third file somebody exported in March that nobody has opened since. The work is pulling those into one place, fixing what is broken inside them, and putting the result on a single page you can read without asking anyone what it means.

I did that in a primary care facility and in an education company. At La Castellana Primary Care Facility I consolidated raw intake and treatment data from 3,300+ emergency room records and built the facility's first interactive dashboard for patient volume, wait times and case outcomes, plus a mortality rate dashboard to watch those trends over time. Neither view existed before. At Kippap Learning Corporation I cleaned LMS and assessment data for 500+ students, resolved data quality issues across 10+ datasets, built 2 Power BI dashboards, stood up a support ticket tracker with real-time KPIs on 60+ cases a month, and sent instructors and stakeholders a weekly report.

Before either of those, my capstone ran from January to November 2025 alongside the degree. That one integrated academic, payment and sentiment data and trained models to flag students at risk of dropping out.

Case 01 · La Castellana Primary Care Facility · May–Aug 2026 · Healthcare

The ER had no dashboard. Then it had one.

Problem

Emergency room intake and treatment data existed only as raw records. There was no interactive view of patient volume, wait times or case outcomes, and no way to watch mortality trends move over time.

Approach

Consolidated the raw intake and treatment data in Google Sheets, then built the facility's first interactive dashboard on top of it, tracking patient volume, wait times and case outcomes. Added a separate mortality rate dashboard to monitor trends across time.

Result

Two working views where there had been none: patient volume, wait times and case outcomes in one place, and mortality trends tracked over time.

Case 02 · Kippap Learning Corporation · Jan–Apr 2026 · Education

Five hundred students, ten broken datasets, one weekly report.

Problem

LMS and assessment data for more than 500 students sat across datasets carrying quality problems, which held back reporting accuracy and consistency. Support cases were being worked without a running record of volume or cause.

Approach

Cleaned and analyzed the LMS and assessment data in MySQL Workbench and Excel/CSV, then resolved the quality issues dataset by dataset. Built 2 Power BI dashboards for engagement, passing rates and performance trends, and a support ticket tracker with real-time KPIs.

Result

Reporting accuracy and consistency improved across the datasets, and instructors and stakeholders received a weekly Power BI report to decide from.

Case 03 · Data Analytics capstone · Jan–Nov 2025 · Education

Flagging the students at risk before they left.

Problem

The signals that a student was about to drop off were split across academic records, payment history and sentiment data, and none of those sources spoke to each other.

Approach

Integrated the academic, payment and sentiment data in Microsoft Access, then trained Random Forest, XGBoost and Logistic Regression models in Python. Shipped the result as a Power BI dashboard where stakeholders filter student risk by term, program and category themselves.

Result

The model reached 90.26% accuracy and an 86.97% F1-score. User acceptance testing returned 100% refresh success and 95.2% user satisfaction.
