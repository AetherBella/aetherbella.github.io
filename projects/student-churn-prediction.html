Student churn prediction

The signs that a student was about to drop out were split across academic records, payment history and sentiment data. My capstone brought them together, trained and compared three models to flag at-risk students early, and put the result in a Power BI dashboard that stakeholders filter themselves.

01Business problem

A school loses students long before the withdrawal form arrives. The early signals (grades, missed payments, how students feel about their experience) lived in academic, payment and sentiment data that sat in separate sources and never spoke to each other. Nobody could see the combined picture early enough to act on it.

02Objectives

  • Can combining academic, payment and sentiment data flag at-risk students early?
  • Which of Random Forest, XGBoost and Logistic Regression predicts churn best on this data?
  • Can stakeholders filter student risk by term, program and risk category themselves, without an analyst?

04Data preparation

  1. IntegrateAcademic, payment and sentiment data joined into one model in Microsoft Access.
  2. TrainRandom Forest, XGBoost and Logistic Regression trained in Python.
  3. CompareModels compared on accuracy and F1-score.
  4. Ship & testResults in a Power BI dashboard, then user acceptance testing.

05Analytical approach

  • Three model families compared: a linear baseline (Logistic Regression) against two tree ensembles (Random Forest, XGBoost).
  • Two measures: accuracy, plus F1-score, which balances precision and recall. Accuracy alone can look good when most students stay.
  • Delivery: a Power BI dashboard that filters predicted risk by term, program and risk category.
  • Validation with users: user acceptance testing on data refresh and satisfaction.

06Results & dashboard

07Business impact

Verified outcomes

  • 90.26% accuracy and 86.97% F1-score in flagging at-risk students.
  • User acceptance testing: 100% data refresh success and 95.2% user satisfaction.
  • A Power BI dashboard stakeholders filter by term, program and risk category themselves.

Potential benefits

  • Reaching at-risk students earlier, while there is still time to help.
  • Focusing retention effort on the programs and terms with the most risk.
