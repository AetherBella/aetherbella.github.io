01Business problem

Emergency room intake and treatment data existed only as raw records. Staff had no interactive view of how many patients came through, how long they waited, or how cases ended, and no data-based way to watch the facility's mortality trends move over time.

Without a single consolidated dataset, every question about the ER meant going back to the raw records by hand.

02Objectives

  • Turn raw intake and treatment records into one clean dataset ready for analysis and reporting.
  • Show patient volume: how many patients the ER sees, and how that changes over time.
  • Show wait times and case outcomes in the same view.
  • Track the mortality rate over time for clinical and operational review.

05Analytical approach

The dashboards track four measures the facility did not previously have in one place:

  • Patient volume over time.
  • Wait times.
  • Case outcomes.
  • Mortality rate, followed as a trend over time in its own dashboard.

06Dashboards

Two working views where there had been none: patient volume, wait times and case outcomes in one interactive ER dashboard, and mortality trends tracked over time in a second.

07Business impact

Verified outcomes

  • The facility's first interactive ER dashboard, where previously only raw records existed.
  • A mortality rate dashboard giving staff a data-based view for clinical and operational review that the facility did not have before.
  • One consolidated, clean dataset ready for further analysis and reporting.

Potential benefits

  • Spotting periods of high patient volume or long waits early.
  • Reviewing mortality trends regularly instead of reconstructing them by hand.
