01Business problem

A motorcycle retailer selling 40 products needs to read a quarter at a glance: total revenue and volume, how sales moved month to month, which categories carry the business, and which products are stars or dead weight. A transaction list cannot answer that quickly.

02Objectives

  • Report revenue, units sold and average transaction value for the quarter.
  • Show monthly and category sales side by side.
  • Name the best- and worst-performing products, and the highest- and lowest-cost products in the catalogue.

04Analytical approach

  • Headline KPIs: total revenue, average transaction value and total units sold for the selected period, with a month selector.
  • Trend and mix: sales transactions by product, total sales and units per month, total sales by category, and number of sales by category.
  • Product extremes: top and lowest performers by units sold; highest- and lowest-cost products.
  • Detail tables: a list of products sold, plus the offered products and their unit prices.

05Dashboard & findings

  • Sport dominates the category mix. It logged 2,060 sales, while Prototype/Concept logged 28.
  • One clear best-seller. The Suzuki GT 380 sold 759 units. The Ace Standard, the weakest product, sold 21.
  • A narrow cost band. The lowest-cost product is the Yamaha SR 250 SE at ₱20,518 and the highest-cost is the DB Motors Pirat LC at ₱39,255. The products-sold table shows orders of 40 units at a time, which is why the average transaction (₱276,313) is several times any single unit's price.

06Business impact

Delivered

  • A single-page view of a full quarter: revenue, volume, monthly trend, category mix and product extremes.

How a retailer could use it

  • Keeping best-sellers like the Suzuki GT 380 in stock.
  • Reviewing slow movers such as the Ace Standard before reordering.
